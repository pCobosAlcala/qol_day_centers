Baseline characteristics by randomised invitation

Sample: all 120 people interviewed at baseline; 91 were invited in one of the eight randomisation rounds and 29 were never invited. Continuous rows show means and binary rows show percentages; Difference is the first group minus the second (percentage points for binary rows). p values come from Welch t-tests for continuous variables and chi-squared tests with continuity correction for binary ones. n is the number of people with data on each variable, first group / second group. One invited person has no Lawton score, Katz score or caregiver record; every other value is observed. Age is measured at the baseline interview, from birth dates. Education is coded 1 to 10 (1 none, 3 primary, 4 lower secondary, 9 university degree, 10 postgraduate). Cared for by a son or daughter refers to the main caregiver, including caregivers recorded as "Hija/o" (son or daughter). WHOQOL-BREF is the mean of the standard 26 items; it and the environmental domain are on the WHO 0 to 100 scale (25 times the item mean minus 1). The largest imbalance is indigenous status (26.4 per cent of the invited, 44.8 per cent of the never invited; 18.5 percentage points, chi-squared p = 0.100, Fisher exact p = 0.069). The gap is 22 to 23 points in the estimation samples (Table A.5). Columns (c) and (d) of Table A.8 control for it. With ten comparisons the Bonferroni threshold for a 5 per cent test is 0.005; no difference passes it under either test (the smallest p value is 0.069).
