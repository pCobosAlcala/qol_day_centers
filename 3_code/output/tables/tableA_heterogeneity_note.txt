Effect of the invitation at endline by baseline characteristics (exploratory)

Sample: the 85 people observed at baseline and at endline (84 in the Lawton and main caregiver splits, where one answer is missing). Within each subgroup the endline score is regressed on its own baseline value and the randomised invitation (ANCOVA, intention to treat, ITT) with robust HC2 standard errors; p is the p value of that ITT. Scores are on the WHO 0 to 100 scale. 'Per cent of control baseline' is the ITT divided by the baseline mean of the never invited people in the same subgroup, so the two subgroups have different bases and their per cents should not be compared with each other; compare subgroups with the difference column, in points. 'Invited who attended' is the share of the subgroup's invited people who attended the centre on more than five days by the endline survey (39 per cent overall, 25 to 50 across subgroups), so a gap in the ITT can reflect a gap in take-up rather than in the effect of attending. IV is the effect of attending, with attendance instrumented by the invitation within the subgroup and the baseline value in both stages; ‡ marks a robust first-stage F below 10 (weak instrument); the first stage includes the outcome's own baseline value, so the F and the flag can differ between the two outcomes for the same subgroup.
'Difference in ITT' is the first subgroup's ITT minus the second's, in points, with its HC2 p value and 95 per cent confidence interval in brackets, from one regression on the whole sample in which the intercept, the baseline slope and the effect of the invitation all differ by subgroup, so it equals the gap between the two ITTs shown. Heterogeneity is judged by this difference column, not by whether either subgroup's own ITT is significant. The analysis is exploratory: 16 differences are tested (8 characteristics, two outcomes), so about 1.6 would fall below p = 0.10 by chance alone; none does, so none survives a Holm adjustment for the 16 tests either (smallest adjusted p = 1.00).
Splits at the median of this sample: age 80.3 years at the baseline interview, baseline Lawton score 4 of 8 activities and baseline WHOQOL-BREF 58.7 points (at or above versus below). Education (last level completed; lower secondary is the Mexican secundaria) and the number of people living with the older adult are split above versus at or below the median (primary; 2 people), because many people sit exactly on it. Main caregiver: the caregiver said the older adult is their parent, or gave the same relation in reverse ('son or daughter'). † fewer than 8 not invited people in the subgroup (3 for 'someone else', 6 for 'men', 6 for 'lower secondary or more'): those estimates rest on very few controls.
